# The eye sets a size, and the world answers

## The law

The README's, which the session had not read: the world is a question, **what fills
this cube of space, at this size?**, and everything that looks asks that one function,
the pixel's ray, a body's feet, the pointer and an edit alike. Its inputs are two: a
cube and a size. Its answer is the world's alone.

**The user's**: the eye is the camera's eye, and there is no other. The word names
nothing else, and nothing is told apart from it as "the camera's eye" beside another.
Without the one who looks nothing else has a meaning, so nothing but the eye says how
coarse anything is read.

Together: **the eye sets a size and the world answers.** The eye's whole part in a
read is to say at what size a place is asked. What was asked at a size is then no
longer the eye's.

## What follows from it

- **The size is by distance from the eye, the same in every direction.** A screen is
  cut into cells of one angle, and a thing of side `s` at distance `d` spans `s / d`
  radians, so the size that fills a cell is the distance times a constant, and nothing
  else of where the thing lies enters. What that buys, each of which a rule that is not
  the same in every direction loses:
  - A turn of the camera in place changes no size, so nothing seen is asked again by
    it. A size taken along the view's axis, or by the screen's place, changes with
    every turn, and the edge of a wide view is drawn at the wrong size.
  - Every band of distance holds about the same number of cells whichever way the eye
    faces, down from a height as along the ground. A frame's cost is then bound to the
    screen's cells and grows with the logarithm of how far the view reaches. A size
    taken by the ground's distance alone draws the ground under a high eye too fine and
    what stands over a near horizon too coarse.
  - Two eyes of one head, or a head that turns, read one world at the same sizes.
- **One eye, because a size must be one.** A reader that set its own size, by where it
  stands or where it is going, asked the same cube at another size than the screen
  drew it. What is seen was then not what is stood on; an answer kept for one was not
  the other's; and the tiles held follow the eye's bands, so the other's size was not
  there to read. With no eye nothing is observed, and a read answers a lack.
- **A kept answer is kept by the cube and the size.** It goes when the world changes in
  what it read, or when the eye sets another size there, and for nothing else of the
  eye. The eye's own node cache is keyed so, by a node and the zooms asked.
- **A body asks its cell at the size the eye sets for it.** The size is the eye's, by
  the distance to the body's own point in three dimensions; the cell then asks the
  world at that one size. A body under 96 units of the eye is asked at the leaves, from
  96 to 192 in cells two units wide, and past 192 no body shows; what is placed is
  asked finer under 48, 24 and 12. So a camera that turns about a standing body asks
  nothing again, and one that zooms asks again at each of those edges.
- **The eye's own rays ask each node at its own size.** A ray from the eye, the
  pointer's or the orbit's line, crosses many sizes; a body's cell is one. Underneath
  is one walk, which takes the sizes it asks.
- **One height.** A body is drawn where it stands. The morning's code kept two, a floor
  at the leaves to stand on and a floor at the eye's size to be drawn on.
- **The light stays as it is.** The sun is where the light's rays come from. It is no
  eye and sets no size. A tile of the light box asks the world at one size, its band,
  and the eye sets it: the box looks down the sun's rays with the eye at its middle, so
  a tile's place in the box is the distance from the eye to that tile's bundle of rays
  in three dimensions, and the band is the size the eye draws at the bundle's nearest
  point. It is the body's form: one thing, one size the eye sets, an answer that is the
  world's and so is kept when the eye moves. The bundle is long, so the size is the
  finest the eye draws anywhere along it: a caster far along it is asked finer than it
  is drawn, two or three zooms where the sun is low or the eye high, never coarser.
  Asking each node of a light ray at its own distance from the eye would draw a far
  caster's shadow at the caster's own size; it would also cast every read tile again on
  every frame the eye moves, some thirty times the tiles a frame casts today, and no
  form was found that keeps one without the other.

## Rejected

- **A second eye of any kind**: a destination read as an eye standing there would read
  it, a walk with no eye read from its own origin, a pan leading an eye of its own.
  They were added to start the app, which waited because the pivot was put only once
  its floor was read and the eye stood only once the pivot was put. A destination is
  put by what is held, a tile that gives its ground exactly, which is no read; the
  camera then has its eye and stands by it.
- **Every node of a body's ray asked by the eye's distance to that node.** It makes a
  floor a function of the eye's exact place, so nothing kept survives a move of the
  camera, and it was first taken for the rule's own cost.
- **A keep by every node's asked zooms.** Exact, and it keeps nothing: a read's large
  nodes take another range on almost any move of the eye though the floor is the same.
- **The light asked as the eye's rays are.** Its band is the eye's size for a bundle,
  measured in three dimensions; it was misread as a rule set apart by the vertical.
- **The band of a body's column for its floor**: a body would stand on cells finer than
  the eye draws there.
- **A place's surface read down its column, each node by the eye's distance**, kept by
  the eye's place: the same form as the body's, left in the markers.
